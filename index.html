Why Kyūshū

Because it holds everything in one island.

Active volcanoes, rice terraces, fishing villages, hot spring towns, mountain shrines, ceramic workshops. Forests so dense they feel ancient. Coastlines that go on forever. In Kyūshū, you don't need to travel far to find contrast.

The traditions here aren't preserved for tourists—they're still lived. Pottery studios that have fired kilns for 400 years. Fishermen who know every reef. Festivals older than the Edo period.

And there's a rhythm here—slower, steadier, closer to the land. A reminder that not everywhere moves at the same speed. Kyūshū taught us that, and we're still learning.

The Other Japan

Kyushu: seven prefectures, thirteen million people, yet still far from the usual tourist path.
A place where nature, traditions, and a slower rhythm shape everyday life.

🌋

Born of Fire

A land sculpted by volcanoes.
From Mount Aso's giant caldera to the ever-active Sakurajima, Kyushu is a landscape that still breathes.

The Onsen Kingdom

Japan's richest concentration of hot springs.
Beppu, Yufuin, and hundreds of hidden valleys where steam and mineral waters shape local life.

Craft as a Way of Life

Arita, Imari, Karatsu — tradition practiced, not displayed.
Ceramics, sake brewing, indigo textiles: centuries-old skills still alive in real workshops.
